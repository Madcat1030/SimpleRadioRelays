# Player experience review

This assessment uses the current recipes, interactions, signal rules and reported field tests. It is not a new survival playtest or a live-audio benchmark.

## What works

The towers give players a reason to build infrastructure between settlements. Increasing a mast, aiming receivers and choosing intermediate sites are visible, understandable improvements. Saved relays keep that infrastructure useful without requiring players to manage chunk loaders. The in-world cards also make a tower's purpose readable without opening an inventory.

The single upgrade slot creates a meaningful choice: an amplifier makes the local tower stronger, while a Focus connects another dimension. The Ender Amplifier adds a distinct long-distance role by resetting power decay without repairing already damaged audio. Narrow dishes and cheaper broad arrays offer another useful tradeoff between reach in one direction and coverage around a settlement.

Keeping Directional standalone matters for players who want better conventional radio towers without adopting the entire relay progression. Optional Foci is also valuable: dimensional rule changes remain an explicit installation choice.

## Where players may struggle

**First success takes a lot of preparation.** A working relay needs an existing antenna/core, a relay, a catalyst, a bank and a tuned card. The relay recipe itself consumes native radio components. That is reasonable for infrastructure, but a new player can spend considerable time crafting before discovering a mounting or tuning mistake. The handbook helps; a short local test before a long expedition is the most useful next piece of guidance.

**A linked tower is not proof of an end-to-end conversation.** Incoming power, decay, channel translation and asymmetric handheld range can each break a round trip. A player can hear a distant station clearly and still be unable to reply. The display reports local readiness and link count, which is useful but cannot tell the player which leg failed. This is the largest remaining usability gap, particularly when troubleshooting means travelling thousands of blocks. A future player-facing signal test would be more valuable than another block type.

**The early upgrades deserve observation in survival.** Copper and Redstone amplifiers are mechanically distinct, but the added cost of the Redstone tier may be small once players are already building radio infrastructure. Copper could become a briefly used or skipped tier. Likewise, a permanent 90% catalyst is a straightforward replacement for a 60% catalyst once available. These are progression questions to measure in play, not grounds for speculative recipe changes.

**Ender amplification can become the standard backbone answer.** Its stronger antenna contribution and decay reset make it desirable on demanding routes. The cost and competition with a Focus keep it from being an automatic choice everywhere, but survival testing should check whether it makes intermediate amplification tiers feel obsolete too quickly.

**Small hit targets and hidden mounting requirements add friction.** Direct insertion is satisfying when it works, but removing the correct card or upgrade can be awkward at a distance. Directional receivers also need clearance and a non-top vertical mast. These restrictions suit the physical models; clear placement feedback matters more than relaxing them.

The current feature set is substantial enough. The next priority should be profiling results and clearer failure feedback, followed by targeted survival balance tests. More mechanics would add learning cost before these existing interactions are fully explained.
